    Takeaway for the room

    People look up AI constantly. ChatGPT was Wikipedia's most-viewed article of 2023, and Gemini was Google's top trending search of 2025. Yet AI stories don't appear in Chartbeat's lists of the most-engaged news stories, and only 8% of Americans read an article that was mainly about AI, even though 52% visited a news page that mentioned it.

                              Where interpretation research points

                              By 2026 the expert consensus had shifted toward caution. Using LLMs to settle what a word means is “at most one tool among several” (Waldon et al., Georgetown Law Journal 2025). Their answers can flip with small changes to the prompt (Purushothama et al., FAccT 2026), and they may “collapse” real ambiguity into a single confident answer (Gur-Arieh et al., 2026).

                              Meanwhile courts are moving to require human verification rather than ban AI outright. Examples: the Tenth Circuit's proposed rule, California SB 574 and the Sixth Circuit in Whiting.

                              Verification labels

                              Each source was either opened and read, or confirmed through several matching search results when the site blocked automated reading. Items in the second group carry a small ◦ search-confirmed mark. Items marked Pending were still undecided on 30 September 2026 and should be re-checked before I present.

                              Reuters, AP, BBC and the Guardian block automated access, so some stories link to AP copy republished by other outlets, to NPR, CNBC or TechCrunch, or to the primary document (court, regulator or company page).

                              The coverage chart

                              GDELT's DOC 2.0 API returns daily counts of matching articles plus the total number of articles it monitored that day. Daily values were summed into months, and the share is matched articles ÷ all monitored articles. GDELT counts articles whose text matches the query. That is broader than headlines alone, so the chart measures how much AI shows up in the news, not the number of front-page headlines.
